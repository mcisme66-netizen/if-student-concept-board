# Research and concept selection

Use this reference when the topic is open, the user asks what currently wins, or a previous batch feels repetitive.

## Start with official sources

- iF Student Award overview and current criteria: https://ifdesign.com/en/if-design-student-award
- iF professional jury criteria: https://ifdesign.com/en/industry-call-for-entries
- iF sustainability criteria: https://ifdesign.com/en/sustainability-if-design-award
- iF winner ranking/projects: https://ifdesign.com/en/if-design-ranking

Verify dates and current rules on the live pages. Prefer individual winner pages with jury statements over reposted boards.

## What to extract from winners

For 8–12 recent winners, capture:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Problem | Is the need specific and observable? |
| Intervention | What single action changes the situation? |
| System | Who supplies, maintains, returns, repairs, or governs it? |
| Dignity | Does it avoid stigma, surveillance, or forced dependency? |
| Positive experience | Is participation engaging rather than punitive? |
| Feasibility | Can a student prototype the critical mechanism? |
| Sustainability | Is there a real material/behavior/service loop? |
| Jury signal | What did the jury explicitly praise? |

## Patterns demonstrated by recent winners

- **Dignity and discretion:** GAIA reframed pregnancy testing around privacy and inclusion.
- **Shared participation:** Bendadi made tactile play inclusive for sighted and visually impaired children together.
- **Strategic simplicity:** Visual Standard Sheet solved a small educational problem with a printable tool.
- **Learning through action:** Growing Minds and Ocean Sustainability made environmental learning participatory.
- **Circular value:** Paper/olive-waste packaging and the carbon-neutral hangtag converted waste into a useful second function.

The transferable lesson is not to imitate these objects. It is to prefer a precise problem, a clear action, a positive relationship, and an explainable resource loop.

## Saturation check

Apply a penalty when a concept is mainly one of these without a strong new mechanism:

- cute pediatric medical device;
- elderly emergency button;
- disaster rescue gadget;
- AI companion or generic sensor plus app;
- transformable multifunction object with no service owner;
- sustainable material with no sourcing, maintenance, or end-of-life logic.

## Five-part concept score

Score each direction from 1–5:

1. **Problem-solving:** observable need, useful intervention, understandable use.
2. **Ethics and experience:** dignity, fairness, privacy, environmental awareness, pleasure.
3. **Solidarity:** strengthens relations or shared goals rather than isolating the user.
4. **Design relevance:** formal potential, usability, technical independence, demonstrable innovation.
5. **Economic sense:** resource efficiency, feasible prototype, plausible operator and maintenance.

Advance concepts scoring at least 18/25 with no score below 3. A lower-scoring but visually attractive concept should not be rendered yet.
