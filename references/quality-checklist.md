# Quality checklist

Run this before delivering a board.

## Concept

- [ ] The user and context are visible within five seconds.
- [ ] One sentence explains the current workaround and why it fails.
- [ ] The concept has one memorable physical or service action.
- [ ] Before, during, after, maintenance, and end-of-life are defined.
- [ ] A responsible actor owns inspection, replenishment, repair, or governance.
- [ ] The concept's non-claims are explicit where safety or ethics require them.

## iF relevance

- [ ] Problem-solving is concrete rather than speculative.
- [ ] The experience protects dignity and avoids stigma.
- [ ] The design strengthens a relationship or shared goal where appropriate.
- [ ] The form follows the mechanism and audience.
- [ ] The concept can be prototyped or meaningfully tested by a student team.
- [ ] Sustainability describes a loop, not a color palette.

## Product and service consistency

- [ ] Geometry, colors, controls, and component count match across views.
- [ ] Every step uses the same product family.
- [ ] No component floats, duplicates, changes scale, or becomes a different design.
- [ ] Safety equipment and maintenance parts appear where needed.
- [ ] Human posture, reach, and environment are plausible.

## Board

- [ ] The chosen layout family matches the project's proof burden.
- [ ] One hero/action dominates; supporting modules do not compete equally.
- [ ] Visual explanation occupies roughly 60–75% of the board.
- [ ] Title and scenario dominate; microtext does not.
- [ ] English copy is concise and grammatical.
- [ ] Steps use verbs and follow a real sequence.
- [ ] Components explain mechanism rather than decorate the page.
- [ ] Exactly three values summarize the project.
- [ ] Footer contains only Category, Concept, Target, and Status unless the user supplies entrant information.
- [ ] No iF logo, fake award, fake university, fake email, QR code, watermark, or invented statistic.
- [ ] Evidence modules use supplied research; otherwise they are explicitly labeled as a validation plan.

## Image generation

- [ ] Input-image roles are explicit.
- [ ] Layout references are not copied as products.
- [ ] Exact text is quoted in the prompt.
- [ ] Avoid list includes topic-specific failure modes.
- [ ] Final selected image is copied into the workspace with a versioned filename.

## Prototype handoff

End the explanation with two or three highest-risk items to validate next. Examples include mechanism life, material cycles, accessibility standards, water/heat pressure, cleaning, network failure, or operator responsibility.
